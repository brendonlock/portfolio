Process

Four steps, every time.

  1. 01 Define the constraint Budget, size, load, deadline. The constraint is the design.
  2. 02 Prototype ugly Cardboard, scrap, hot glue. Find out what's wrong before it's expensive.
  3. 03 Measure everything If it isn't written down, it didn't happen. Test before the pretty version.
  4. 04 Document and finish Drawings, notes, and a write-up someone else could build from.

Where it's heading

Turbomachinery, and the fastest engineering problem on earth.

The part of mechanical engineering I keep coming back to is turbomachinery: compressors, turbines, and the fluid dynamics that make a spinning blade do useful work. It's where thermodynamics, materials, and precision manufacturing collide in one component, and where a tenth of a percent of efficiency is worth chasing for years.

Formula 1 is the sharpest version of that. A hybrid power unit that's among the most thermally efficient engines ever built, a turbocharger spinning past a hundred thousand revolutions a minute, and aerodynamics rethought between races. I follow it for the engineering as much as the racing, and it's the direction I want my degree to point.
